# Credibility without a portfolio: how a new UK commercial solar installer (ex-subcontractor founders) can present an honest, convincing website

Research date: 28 September 2026. UK focus; non-UK sources are flagged. Where a point is legal-advice territory it is marked **[solicitor to confirm]**; where it is settled regulatory practice (ASA/CAP, MCS scheme documents) it is marked **[settled]**.

Context assumed from the brief: two brothers, qualified electricians, 3+ years installing commercial solar PV as subcontractors to another installer; they appear already to run an electrical contracting business (Murray Electrical); the new company will offer commercial solar installs plus PPAs. Nothing on the founders' actual subcontract terms was available to me, so every contract-dependent point below is conditional on what their agreement says.

## Key Question 1 — Legal/ethical: what can a former subcontractor say online about projects they worked on?

### Takeaway
UK advertising rules (CAP Code, applying to websites and to B2B marketing) let the founders describe their genuine, documentable experience, provided the overall impression is not misleading: "our founders have installed X MWp as subcontractors to a national installer" is defensible if they hold evidence and disclose the subcontract role; "we delivered these projects" or "established since [electrical-business year]" is not. Copyright in site photos normally belongs to whoever took them, but confidentiality clauses (e.g. NEC4 cl. 29 publicity restriction), site rules and UK GDPR mean photos and client names should only be published with written permission. Non-solicitation clauses of 3–12 months targeted at clients actually dealt with are commonly enforceable; broad non-competes are harder to enforce, but the subcontract must be read **[solicitor to confirm]**.

### Cited Findings

**Advertising rules (CAP Code) — [settled]**
- CAP Code Rule 3.7: marketers must "hold documentary evidence to prove claims that consumers are likely to regard as objective and that are capable of objective substantiation"; "objective claims must be supported by objective evidence; self-reported consumer data or other subjective data will not be sufficient" — [ASA/CAP Substantiation advice](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/substantiation.html)
- The ASA judges "the likely interpretation of a claim, rather than the marketer's intention", so even intended puffery needs evidence if readers will take it as objective — [ASA/CAP Substantiation advice](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/substantiation.html)
- Rule 3.1: "Marketing communications must not materially mislead or be likely to do so"; Rule 3.3: must not "omit material information"; Rule 3.11: must not exaggerate capability or performance. The ASA considers "the overall impression those ads are likely to give consumers, as well as the specific claims and images". Company websites are covered (example: NOW TV homepage ruling). "Marketers wishing to run business-to-business marketing must comply with the Business Protection from Misleading Marketing Regulations 2008 as well as the CAP Code" — [ASA/CAP Misleading advertising advice](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/misleading-advertising.html)
- Rule 3.2 permits obvious exaggeration that consumers won't take literally; Rule 3.6: marketers must "not mislead by implying that expressions of opinion are objective claims" (e.g. Sky's "the perfect network" was treated as objective and needed evidence) — [ASA/CAP Puffery advice](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/types-of-claims-puffery-and-expressions-of-opinion.html)
- Testimonials: Rule 3.47 — marketers "must hold documentary evidence to show that a testimonial or endorsement used in a marketing communication is genuine" and hold contact details; Rule 3.48 — testimonials must relate to the advertised product/service and not be edited misleadingly; Rule 3.49 — claims within a testimonial must not mislead; Rule 3.50 — "Marketing communications must not feature a testimonial without permission"; Rule 3.52 — no trust/quality marks or claimed approvals without authorisation; Rule 3.53 — no false claims of code membership. Acceptable evidence includes signed testimonials with contact details and email correspondence — [ASA/CAP Testimonials and endorsements advice](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/testimonials-and-endorsements.html)
- Precedent on "history" claims: Coys advertised "Founded 1919"/"since 1919"; the ASA found the words were "likely to be read as providing assurance to potential clients and customers that Coys had a long trading history, denoting financial stability and inherited goodwill and expertise", when the trading entity was incorporated in 2003 after predecessor liquidations. CAP Copy Advice on "established since" claims notes consumers "might be persuaded by claims that a company is well-established or has a long history, especially in industries that might have a high level of failed or bankrupted companies". The High Court refused judicial review (2012) — [Osborne Clarke marketinglaw](https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/advertising-regulation/founded-1919-classic-car-business-challenges-asa/); [Simkins](https://www.simkins.com/news/high-court-coy-about-overruling-asa-s-expertise)
- MCS Installer Agreement (v1.0, 1 Sept 2025) cl. 29: installers must not engage in conduct "reasonably likely to cause material harm to the reputation of Us, the MCS Certification Mark, or the Scheme", including "knowingly making false or misleading statements regarding Your certification status or the Scheme"; cl. 26: use the Mark only per brand guidelines; cl. 27: if suspended/terminated, "Remove all references to MCS certification in Your marketing materials" — [MCS Installer Agreement PDF](https://mcscertified.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/MCS-Installer-Agreement-1.0-1.9.2025.pdf)
- A UK commercial-buyer guide lists "No MCS certification (or 'we're applying')" as a kill-the-tender red flag, alongside "Lack of commercial references" — [Solar Panels for Businesses, How to choose a commercial solar installer (26 Mar 2026, upd. 2 May 2026)](https://solarpanelsforbusinesses.co.uk/blog/how-to-choose-commercial-solar-installer-uk-business/)
- Solarable (29 Jul 2026) states that under the redeveloped MCS scheme "Online presence now audited during scheme checks for misleading claims and customer feedback" — [Solarable MCS timeline guide](https://solarable.org/guides/mcs-certification-timeline-small-installer) (single secondary source; not found in the MCS documents I could read)

**Confidentiality / publicity under construction contracts — [solicitor to confirm for their actual subcontract]**
- NEC4 introduced "new clause 29 'Disclosure' which restricts the contractor's ability to publicise the works without the client's agreement" — [C-Link, NEC3 vs NEC4](https://c-link.com/blog/nec3-vs-nec4/); NEC4 ECC clause 29 comprises 29.1 "Non-Disclosure of Information" and 29.2 "Publicity Restriction" — [nec4engine clause index](https://www.nec4engine.com/clauses); "A new core clause deals with confidentiality, restricting the disclosure of project information" — [Designing Buildings, NEC4](https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/NEC4)
- Under the NEC4 Engineering and Construction Subcontract, cl. 22.1 gives the client and contractor rights to "use and copy" the subcontractor's design; with Option X9 ownership of design material transfers to the contractor; cl. 29.1 prohibits disclosure of information except as needed to perform the contract — [NEC, How the NEC4 ECS deals with IP (27 Oct 2025)](https://www.neccontract.com/news/how-the-nec4-ecs-deals-with-intellectual-property-rights)
- Sprintlaw (UK) guidance on subcontractor agreements: showing completed work in a portfolio "can be completely normal but should be agreed upfront"; confidentiality clauses requiring information to be used "only for the project" are standard where subcontractors see client information, pricing or plans — [Sprintlaw, Subcontractor agreement key terms](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/subcontractor-agreement-in-the-uk-key-terms-to-include/) (from search summary of the Sprintlaw series)

**Naming the main contractor / clients (passing off, trade marks) — [solicitor to confirm]**
- Passing off requires (1) goodwill, (2) a misrepresentation in the course of trade leading the public to believe the defendant's services are connected with the claimant, and (3) damage or likelihood of damage; it protects names, logos and get-up — [LexisNexis, UK Passing Off](https://www.lexisnexis.com/en-gb/legal/guidance/introduction-to-passing-off)
- Truthful nominative reference to another brand ("for use with EPSON") "is often acceptable... when done truthfully and with appropriate clarity", but "using the EPSON logo prominently... or styling your brand to look like an official accessory, can tip into infringement" — [Sprintlaw, Trademark infringement examples UK](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/trademark-infringement-examples-in-the-uk/)

**Photographs taken on site — copyright vs the right to publish**
- "The author or creator of the work is usually the first owner"; where a work is created "in the course of his employment" the employer is first owner; commissioned works belong to the creator "unless you otherwise agree it in writing"; transfers must be in writing — [GOV.UK, Ownership of copyright works](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/ownership-of-copyright-works)
- For contractors/freelancers "the contractor who creates original works for you will own the copyright in them" unless assigned in a signed written contract — [nibusinessinfo, Contractors and copyright](https://www.nibusinessinfo.co.uk/content/contractors-and-copyright); [Sprintlaw, Who owns copyright in a photograph](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/who-owns-copyright-in-a-photograph/)
- Posting photos engages UK GDPR (identifiable people are personal data; need a lawful basis, consent often safest for marketing), breach of confidence (e.g. "client files visible on a desk"), and private-premises expectations; recommended: written consent forms, checks for background data (screens, documents, addresses), and takedown procedures — [Sprintlaw, UK laws on posting photos without consent for businesses](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/uk-laws-on-posting-photos-without-consent-for-businesses/)
- Contractor photo/case-study checklist (Canada, 27 Jul 2026 — non-UK but practice is transferable): record for every asset who created it and the rights basis; obtain separate project/property authorisation covering whether the customer may be named, channels, edits, duration and removal; "Do not imply responsibility for an entire renovation when the contractor completed one trade"; confirm who is "allowed to capture media on the site"; preserve customer quotes verbatim and get approval for the exact excerpt — [Nexxen Solutions checklist](https://nexxensolutions.ca/resources/contractor-project-photos-case-study-permission-checklist)

**Non-solicitation / non-compete clauses — [solicitor to confirm]**
- UK test: clauses must "go no further than reasonably necessary to protect your 'legitimate business interests'", be limited in time, scope and geography; non-competes "are harder to enforce in the UK and must be tightly drafted to avoid being void as a 'restraint of trade'"; non-solicitation is more enforceable because it restricts only poaching; typical durations "commonly 3–12 months", longer "rarely upheld unless justified"; restrict to "clients or staff that the person actually worked with" — [Sprintlaw, Non-solicitation clauses in UK business contracts (18 Sep 2025)](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/understanding-non-solicitation-clauses-in-uk-business-contracts-a-guide-for-employers-and-entrepreneurs/)
- Courts uphold restrictive covenants only when "clear, proportionate and justified"; restrictions beyond 6–12 months face significant challenge; enforcement by injunction typically costs £10,000+ — [Hybrid Legal (13 Oct 2025)](https://www.hybridlegal.co.uk/blog/restrictive-covenants-non-solicitation-clauses-a-reality-check-for-uk-businesses)

### Inferences
- **"Our team has installed over X MWp across Y commercial sites"** is an objective claim under Rule 3.7 and must be backed by records (timesheets, invoices, commissioning sheets, DNO/G99 paperwork, dated photos, panel counts). It is acceptable *if* the sentence makes the subcontract role clear (otherwise it risks misleading by omission under 3.3 and by "overall impression" under 3.1). Not naming clients or the main contractor is fine — vagueness about *who* is not misleading, but vagueness about *the company's role* is.
- Attribution language: "worked on", "installed as part of a team", "as subcontractor to a national installer", "our founders' experience includes..." keep the claim personal to the founders. "We delivered", "our project", "our portfolio", "our clients include [main contractor's clients]" attribute the work to the new company and are the risky phrases. The Coys principle also means "established since [Murray Electrical founding year]" or "X years in business" for the new solar entity would be misleading unless the solar business trades as the same legal entity.
- Because the CAP Code applies to B2B marketing plus the BPRs 2008, "it's B2B so consumer rules don't apply" is not a defence.
- **Written permission from the main contractor is advisable** (not strictly required for truthful, non-confidential statements, but it removes the confidentiality/publicity-clause risk, the passing-off/implied-endorsement risk and the relationship risk). A short request should: (1) name the specific projects/photos/figures to be used; (2) offer neutral wording ("installed by [Founders] as subcontractors to [Main Contractor]" or "to a national installer"); (3) commit not to name end clients without their separate consent; (4) commit to remove on request; (5) ask for a reference contact. Suggested skeleton (my drafting, not sourced): "Dear [MC], as you know [we] are setting up [NewCo]. We would like to reference, on our website, the commercial PV work we carried out for you as subcontractors between [dates], using the aggregate figures [X MWp / Y sites], the attached [n] photographs (no client names, signage or people shown), and the wording: '[proposed sentence]'. We will not name [MC] or its clients unless you confirm you are happy for us to do so, and we will remove any item at your request. Would you be willing to confirm this in writing, and could [site manager] provide a short reference on our workmanship?"
- **Photographs**: if the founders took the photos themselves while working as individuals/subcontractors, they (or their company, if taken as its employees) probably own the copyright; but ownership does not override confidentiality or publicity clauses, site photography rules or GDPR. Practical rule: publish only photos that (a) they took, (b) show only their own work, (c) contain no identifiable people, signage, site names or documents, and (d) the main contractor has cleared in writing. Client-owned drone shots, main contractor marketing images and anything from the main contractor's monitoring portal should not be used.
- **Non-solicitation**: the founders should check their subcontract for (i) confidentiality, (ii) publicity/photo clauses, (iii) non-solicitation of clients/staff, (iv) non-compete, and (v) IP assignment. A clause stopping them approaching the main contractor's end clients for 6–12 months is plausibly enforceable; a blanket ban on doing commercial solar is less likely to be. Marketing generally (website, SEO, ads) is normally not "solicitation", but directly approaching the main contractor's clients could be **[solicitor to confirm]**.
- Testimonials from the main contractor's site managers are permissible if the person consents (3.50) and the quote is genuine and held on file (3.47); if the quote is given in a personal capacity, say so, and avoid implying the main contractor corporately endorses the new company (3.52 spirit).

## Key Question 2 — Credibility substitutes: accreditations, memberships, manufacturer programmes, insurance and guarantees a new UK firm can actually obtain

### Takeaway
For UK commercial buyers the non-negotiables are electrical registration (NICEIC/NAPIT), MCS (obtainable in roughly 2–4 months if a qualified electrician has a compliant ≤50 kWp install to be assessed on; ~£1,044 first-year via NICEIC), evidence of £2m–£5m+ public liability plus professional indemnity, an SSIP health-and-safety badge (CHAS/SafeContractor from ~£429–£540/yr, days–weeks), IPAF/PASMA/CSCS cards, and an insurance-backed workmanship warranty. Consumer codes (RECC/HIES) and TrustMark are domestic-only and no longer mandatory for MCS; Constructionline and ISO need trading history/6–12 months and can wait. Several manufacturer certifications (Enphase, Fronius, SolaX, Tesla, Huawei) are open to new firms via free or low-cost training, but "Gold/Platinum" tiers depend on installed volume.

### Cited Findings

**MCS (Solar PV) — [settled, from MCS documents]**
- Official steps: understand Standards; build a team with three roles — Main Contact, Licensee (signs the Installer Agreement), Technical Supervisor (ensures compliance); set up processes; and "for each technology you wish to become certified for, you will need to complete at least one fully compliant installation" for certification-body assessment; nine certification bodies across technologies — [MCS, Becoming certified](https://mcscertified.com/installers/becoming-certified/)
- MIS 3002:2025 scope: design, installation and commissioning of solar PV systems "supplying permanent buildings and normally connected in parallel to the electricity distribution network up to a maximum DC output of 50kWp"; Appendix C contains a model handover document/"PV System Verification Certificate" adapted from BS EN 62446-1 — [MIS 3002:2025 PDF](https://mcscertified.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/MIS-3002-2025-V1.0.pdf)
- MCS Installer Agreement cl. 24: installers must hold "Public Liability Insurance with a minimum coverage of two million pounds (£2,000,000)", "Professional Indemnity Insurance sufficient to cover any liabilities arising from any professional services You provide", and Employer's Liability where applicable; cl. 21: for domestic contracts an MCS-approved financial protection product must be bought within 7 days of the certificate — [MCS Installer Agreement PDF](https://mcscertified.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/MCS-Installer-Agreement-1.0-1.9.2025.pdf)
- NICEIC (Certsure) MCS route: Technical Supervisors need an "in-date qualification, in-line with the requirements in the Installer Operating Requirements"; insurance minimums stated as £2m PL and £250,000 PI; 2026/27 first-year fees: application £114 + certification (one technology) £846 + MCS licence £66 = £1,044 inc. VAT; initial assessment "approx 3 hours" office and site per Technical Supervisor per technology; annual surveillance reviews "minimum of one installation of each technology" — [NICEIC MCS scheme page](https://niceic.com/for-the-trades-1/professional-standards/schemes/mcs-scheme/)
- Realistic timeline for an already-qualified electrician with an installation ready: "2–4 months" (role set-up 1–3 weeks; CB application and MCS checks 3–6 weeks; assessment and non-conformities 2–6 weeks); five solar PV certification bodies: Amtivo, Certsure (NICEIC), NAPIT, Simply Certification, The IAA; Technical Supervisor must hold "an in-date qualification, or an in-date personnel certification in accordance with ISO/IEC 17024"; routes to a first installation include working "as subcontractor through MCS-certified umbrella scheme" or installing for a customer who does not need SEG; RECC membership "no longer mandatory" — [Solarable, MCS timeline (29 Jul 2026)](https://solarable.org/guides/mcs-certification-timeline-small-installer)
- Costs: certification body initial fees "£500 to £1,500", annual packages "£800 to £1,200"; "£30 per installation" MCS registration; Level 3 PV award small if the Technical Supervisor is already an electrician; consumer code now "a commercial decision rather than a compliance cost" — [Solarable, MCS cost (29 Jul 2026)](https://solarable.org/guides/mcs-certification-cost-uk-installers)
- Redeveloped scheme: new installers "stay on standard frequency for their first two years" (annual assessments); every domestic installation needs an MCS-approved financial protection product of at least six years; all installers must transition by 31 March 2027 — [Reonic, MCS Redeveloped Installer Scheme (15 Jul 2026)](https://reonic.com/en-gb/blog/mcs-redeveloped-installer-scheme/); [APHC MCS 2025 update](https://aphc.co.uk/news/mcs-2025-update/)
- Qualification pathway: Level 3 PV awards are City & Guilds 2922-34, EAL Level 3 Award in Installation of Small-Scale Solar PV Systems, or LCL Awards Level 3 — "3–5 days" for qualified electricians; prerequisites NVQ Level 3, AM2/AM2E, 18th Edition incl. Section 712, ECS Gold Card; recommended extras: 2391 inspection & testing, EESS battery qualification, PASMA (1 day) or IPAF (1–2 days), CSCS card — [elec.training pathway (reviewed 6 Jan 2026)](https://elec.training/news/how-to-become-a-solar-pv-installer-uk-solar-installation-pathway-2026/); C&G 2399 is being succeeded by 2922-34 — [Trade Skills 4U](https://www.tradeskills4u.co.uk/courses/city-guilds-2399-course)

**Insurance and guarantees**
- Commercial buyer guide (2026): expect "£5m+ public liability" and "£5m+ professional indemnity insurance", genuine policies not "installer balance sheet"; also lists "IPAF + PASMA tickets" and, for due diligence, "References from at least 3 commercial installs of similar size in the last 18 months", PVsyst P50/P90 yield models, itemised quotes and ENA constraints checks — [Solar Panels for Businesses, Commercial solar installers UK 2026](https://solarpanelsforbusinesses.co.uk/commercial-solar-installers-uk/)
- Clients should "require contractors to provide evidence of public liability and professional indemnity insurance before commencing work"; insurers expect certified installers, building-regs/fire compliance, structural engineering reports and maintenance records — [Eden Sustainable (Jun 2024, upd. Sep 2025)](https://www.edensustainable.co.uk/news/what-insurance-considerations-are-there-for-commercial-solar-panels)
- Most solar contractors buy £1m–£5m public liability; commercial projects may require more — [Simply Business](https://www.simplybusiness.co.uk/business-insurance/solar-panel-installer-insurance/) (search summary)
- Insurance-backed warranty (IWA/IBG) is "the one that matters most for commercial buyers" because it protects the client if the installer fails during a 10-year workmanship warranty; schemes named: HIES, GGF Insurance, QANW, MCS-administered options — [Solar Panels for Businesses, How to choose](https://solarpanelsforbusinesses.co.uk/blog/how-to-choose-commercial-solar-installer-uk-business/)

- G99: applies above 16 A per phase (~3.68 kW single-phase, ~11 kW three-phase), so "almost every commercial solar PV installation" needs G99 approval before installation; the installer normally manages the whole process — [Solar Panels for Businesses, G99](https://solarpanelsforbusinesses.co.uk/g99-application/); [Reonic G99 guide](https://reonic.com/en-gb/blog/g99-application-dno-approval-guide/)

### Inferences
- For a *commercial-only* firm the credibility stack that buyers actually check is: NICEIC/NAPIT → MCS → insurance certificates (PL £5m is the safe commercial default; PI at a level a funder will accept) → SSIP badge → IPAF/PASMA/CSCS → IBG on every job → manufacturer certifications. RECC/HIES/TrustMark/Which?/Checkatrade are domestic signals and can be skipped unless they add domestic work (one buyer guide disagrees, so holding RECC is harmless but not essential).
- MCS is technically scoped to ≤50 kWp (MIS 3002), so most large commercial roofs fall outside MCS's technical scope — yet buyer guides still treat MCS as a walk-away criterion. The website should therefore show MCS as soon as it is held, and until then say nothing about MCS (not even "applying") — buyers read "we're applying" as a red flag and the MCS Agreement forbids misleading statements about certification status.
- MCS is achievable within a 90-day plan only if the first compliant ≤50 kWp installation happens in the first 4–6 weeks (assessment is on a real install). This makes the "first case study" and "MCS assessment install" the same project.
- Manufacturer programmes give fast, honest badges: Enphase (free, hours), Fronius (free one-day course; partner status needs registered inverters), SolaX Silver (associate training), Tesla (online training, no minimum orders per the DACH source). Higher tiers are volume-based, so the site should say "Enphase-certified installer" rather than "Enphase Platinum partner". Do not feature GivEnergy.
- Constructionline and ISO should be scheduled for year 2 (accounts/trading-history dependent), unless a specific tender demands them.
- Solar Energy UK complementary affiliate membership (MCS + turnover <£500k) is a free association signal once MCS is held; logo-use rights were not confirmed and should be checked with the association.

### Gaps
- No public UK criteria found for SolarEdge, SMA, Huawei-UK, JA Solar, Longi, Trina, Canadian Solar, Aiko, Van der Valk, Schletter or Renusol installer programmes; K2 Pro appears US-only. These need direct enquiries via UK distributors.
- Whether IBG providers (QANW, HIES, GGF) will underwrite non-domestic installations by a company with no trading history could not be confirmed (Reonic's guide is domestic-only; QANW page inaccessible). Ask providers directly.
- Specific PI limits that PPA funders require were not found; UrbanVolt lists design/equipment/HSE/O&M requirements but not insurance limits (see KQ3).
- The MCS Installer Operating Requirements PDF (which sets the exact Technical Supervisor qualification list) returned 404; the NAPIT assessment guidance PDF could not be text-extracted.

## Key Question 3 — Content strategy: what replaces a portfolio, and what do commercial buyers actually weigh?

### Takeaway
UK buyer guides and B2B trust research converge: buyers weigh verifiable competence (MCS/NICEIC, insurance, IBG), *reference-able* experience (three similar commercial references in the last 18–24 months is the stated benchmark), engineering transparency (half-hourly load modelling, PVsyst P50/P90 yields, structural and G99 process) and post-commissioning support. Peer evidence beats vendor copy (>90% of B2B buyers trust peers more than vendor content). Founder bios with quantified, substantiated experience, a transparent process page, sample design outputs and modelled "example system" pages are legitimate stand-ins, but they must be labelled as modelled, not delivered.

### Cited Findings
- Reference questions buyers are told to ask previous clients: performance vs modelled forecast, timeline adherence, cost overruns, post-commissioning support responsiveness, willingness to rehire; "The fourth question—operational support post-commissioning—is the most predictive of installer quality"; for projects above £100,000, "request three commercial reference projects completed within 24 months of similar scale"; other red flags: no half-hourly load modelling for >100 kW, undisclosed component specs, unnamed warranty backing, deposits >30%, unrealistic DNO timelines, inflated self-consumption claims — [Solar Panels for Businesses, How to choose (2026)](https://solarpanelsforbusinesses.co.uk/blog/how-to-choose-commercial-solar-installer-uk-business/)
- Due-diligence markers: itemised quotes ("12 line items minimum"), "PVSyst yield model with P50 and P90 estimates", payback/discounted payback/IRR/NPV, ENA constraints check before pricing, IBG documentation, "References from at least 3 commercial installs of similar size in the last 18 months" — [Solar Panels for Businesses, Commercial installers UK 2026](https://solarpanelsforbusinesses.co.uk/commercial-solar-installers-uk/)
- A UK commercial installer's own buyer guidance: look for MCS plus NICEIC, CHAS, Constructionline and RECC; examine "blogs or case studies on previous projects, in conjunction with any sector-specific installations"; expect "a full site survey including roof structure, shading, orientation, ventilation and grid-connection constraints" and "full hand-over documentation: commissioning data, performance expectations, maintenance schedule" — [DMB Solar (11 Nov 2025)](https://www.dmbsolar.co.uk/how-to-choose-a-solar-installer/)
- B2B trust: "Over 90% of respondents said they trust peers in their industry more than vendor content" (Forrester 2023, as cited); "55% of B2B buyers examine reviews of 3–5 vendors before making contact" (B2B SaaS Reviews 2023, as cited) — [Trustmary (27 Oct 2025)](https://trustmary.com/social-proof/how-trust-elements-impact-b2b-buying-decisions/)
- "94% of B2B buyers who used AI during their purchase journey" fact-checked it; peer reviews, independent publications and documented customer experiences are the third-party signals AI models weight — [MarketScale on TrustRadius](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/software-and-technology/94-of-b2b-buyers-fact-check-ai-research-before-trusting-it-trustradius-finds)
- Aggregated B2B buying statistics claim 68% of buyers have a front-runner at the start and that vendor wins ~80% of the time — [Omnibound](https://www.omnibound.ai/blog/b2b-buying-statistics) (aggregator; underlying survey not verified)
- US C&I guidance (non-UK): buyers should compare vendors on "engineering depth, experience with similar project types, responsiveness and post installation support" — [Vikta Energy](https://www.viktaenergy.com/news/the-complete-guide-to-choosing-the-right-commercial-amp-industrial-solar-contractor-and-why-it-matters-more-than-you-think) (search summary)
- PPA funder (UK/IE) requirements of EPC partners: "Smart, optimised system design for dependable energy yield", Tier 1 panels/inverters with extended warranties and full connectivity, "Compliance with regional HSE standards, with UrbanVolt retaining oversight and stop-work authority during installation", "Clear pricing, staged payments, and full traceability on all major equipment", "Two years of preventative maintenance included"; EPCs enter designs in a partner portal for indicative PPA pricing and the funder handles contracting, funding and monitoring — [UrbanVolt, How solar EPCs unlock funded pipeline with a PPA](https://urbanvolt.com/guides/how-solar-epcs-unlock-funded-pipeline-with-a-ppa/)
- A UK funded-solar framework states every installer completes "a PAS 91 pre-qualification questionnaire" covering competence, capability and financials — [MTFD](https://mtfd.co.uk/solar-for/leisure/in/n10) (from search summary; not visible on the fetched page); a PPA provider directory says EPC requirements "include MCS and NICEIC accreditation, and a credible 20-year performance warranty" — [Solar PPA providers UK](https://solarpowerpurchaseagreements.co.uk/providers/) (search summary)
- MIS 3002 Appendix C model handover: PV System Verification Certificate listing client, description, rated power (kW DC), estimated system performance, test data etc. — [MIS 3002:2025](https://mcscertified.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/MIS-3002-2025-V1.0.pdf)
- A newly-founded UK installer states its founding principle and team experience rather than a portfolio: "We're not a sales company. We're a team of installers, electricians, and engineers with decades of hands-on experience in solar and electrical work", alongside accreditation logos, a 5.0 Google rating and a 25-year performance warranty — [Generate Solar EV](https://generate-solarev.co.uk/)

### Inferences
- **Founder bios** should carry the substantiated numbers (years on the tools, MWp, sites, panel counts, roof types — trapezoidal, standing seam, membrane, ballasted flat roof, ground-mount — inverter/optimiser families, G99 applications handled), each phrased as the founders' experience "as subcontractors". Keep a private evidence file per number (Rule 3.7).
- **Process transparency page** maps directly to what buyers are told to check: half-hourly data analysis → site/structural survey → ENA/DNO G99 pre-application → PVsyst/PV*SOL design with P50/P90 → itemised quotation → CDM/RAMS → install → BS EN 62446 commissioning and MIS 3002-style handover pack → O&M with response times. Publishing a redacted sample handover pack and a sample commissioning certificate is strong evidence of competence without a portfolio.
- **Example-system pages** (e.g. "100 kWp on a 2,000 m² warehouse roof in the Midlands") using design-tool outputs are acceptable if clearly labelled "modelled example, not an installed system" and the assumptions (irradiance, yield, tariff, self-consumption) are stated; unlabelled figures would be misleading by omission (3.3) and exaggeration (3.11).
- For PPAs, the website should show the funder-facing basics buyers and funders check (Tier 1 kit with named warranties, O&M included, HSE compliance, staged payments, monitoring), and name a funding partner only once a partnership agreement exists.
- Because peers beat vendor copy, every page should route to third-party evidence (LinkedIn recommendations, Google reviews, reference contacts available on request) rather than adjectives.

### Gaps
- No UK survey of facilities managers/finance directors ranking installer-selection criteria was found; the evidence is buyer guides (secondary) and general B2B trust research (partly US/SaaS-centric).
- No source specifically evaluated "example system"/modelled pages as a substitute for case studies; the treatment above is inferred from CAP rules.

## Key Question 4 — Social proof without projects (testimonials, references, LinkedIn, Google reviews, the existing electrical business)

### Takeaway
Genuine, permissioned testimonials from main-contractor site managers and from the founders' electrical-contracting customers can be used, but each must relate to the service it describes (electrical work labelled as electrical work, PV work as PV work), be held on file with contact details, and not imply corporate endorsement by the main contractor. Google reviews survive a business-name change if the existing profile is edited rather than a new one created — but only if the solar company genuinely is the same business; otherwise the Coys principle applies.

### Cited Findings
- CAP rules 3.47–3.50 (genuine, evidenced, relevant to the advertised product, with permission) — [ASA/CAP Testimonials advice](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/testimonials-and-endorsements.html)
- "Founded 1919"-type claims mislead when the trading entity is new — [Osborne Clarke on Coys](https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/advertising-regulation/founded-1919-classic-car-business-challenges-asa/)
- Google: reviews are tied to the profile, not the name; editing the name on the existing profile keeps reviews, while creating a new listing loses them; Google can move reviews between profiles in defined cases — [Google Business Profile Help, Move your reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3098204?hl=en); [SOCi](https://www.soci.ai/knowledge-articles/how-to-keep-your-reviews-after-a-business-name-change/); [Birdeye](https://birdeye.com/blog/google-my-business-name-change/)
- Which? Trusted Traders positions its endorsement (profile with customer reviews and logo) as a reputation-builder for new installers — [Which? Trusted Traders (5 Nov 2024)](https://for-traders.which.co.uk/advice/how-to-become-a-solar-panel-installer) (consumer-facing)
- Customer quotes: "Preserve original wording and source date", obtain authorisation for the exact excerpt, document name/role/company attribution, "Never combine parts of different messages or write words for the customer" — [Nexxen checklist (Canada)](https://nexxensolutions.ca/resources/contractor-project-photos-case-study-permission-checklist)
- Established UK commercial contractor Unitary Engineering relies on client logos (Skanska, CBRE, Veolia, First Bus, UWE, English Heritage), five named case studies and "We commit to responding to all enquiries within 24 hours" — [Unitary Engineering](https://unitaryengineering.co.uk/) (shows the bar the new firm is competing against)

### Inferences
- Site-manager/project-manager references from the main contractor are the single most relevant third-party proof of *commercial PV* competence; ask for them in a personal capacity, in writing, with permission to publish name and role (or "Site Manager, national solar installer" if the employer prefers anonymity). LinkedIn recommendations are naturally personal and publicly verifiable — encourage the founders to collect them on their personal profiles and link to them.
- Murray Electrical's reviews/testimonials can be shown under a clearly-labelled "Our electrical contracting track record" block ("The founders also run Murray Electrical, est. [year]; reviews below relate to that business"). They should not be merged into a "solar reviews" carousel (3.48) and the solar company should not claim Murray Electrical's founding year as its own unless it is the same legal entity/trading name.
- Client testimonials from the main contractor's end clients require both the client's and (practically) the main contractor's permission; without both, do not use.

### Gaps
- No source addressed the specific situation of subcontractor founders obtaining testimonials from a main contractor; the guidance above is inferred from CAP rules and permission best practice.

## Key Question 5 — Bootstrapping a portfolio fast, and what a professional case study contains

### Takeaway
Three to five well-documented small projects beat a vague experience claim; the fastest honest routes are (1) a ≤50 kWp install on the founders' own or a friendly business's premises that doubles as the MCS assessment install, (2) continuing to subcontract through an MCS-certified installer while negotiating written case-study rights, (3) at-cost installs for community buildings, and (4) O&M/inspection/testing jobs that generate reports and references. A case study should follow situation → complication → solution → outcome, 200–300 words, with kWp, panel count, kit, annual generation, self-consumption, savings, payback, CO2, a client quote and dated photos.

### Cited Findings
- "Five to ten well-documented, diverse projects — each with photos, context, and a client testimonial — will outperform a gallery of 50 unlabeled finished photos"; "Three well-documented small projects are more convincing than a vague claim of '10 years of experience'"; structure: situation, complication, solution, outcome; 200–300 words; testimonial tied to the specific project — [TaskTag (US, 7 Apr 2026)](https://blog.tasktag.com/portfolio-ideas-for-general-contractors)
- Routes to the first MCS-assessable installation: "Work as subcontractor through MCS-certified umbrella scheme" or "Install for customer not requiring Smart Export Guarantee eligibility"; the installation "cannot be done retrospectively" — [Solarable MCS timeline](https://solarable.org/guides/mcs-certification-timeline-small-installer)
- MCS assessment requires "at least one fully compliant installation" per technology — [MCS, Becoming certified](https://mcscertified.com/installers/becoming-certified/)
- UK start-up guidance emphasises networking ("Building relationships with other businesses and organisations in the renewable energy industry" via trade shows and associations), brand, customer feedback and after-sales follow-up — [eWorks Manager](https://www.eworksmanager.co.uk/how-to-start-your-own-solar-installation-business/); legal essentials list: client contracts with specs/timelines/warranties, subcontractor agreements, privacy policy, PL/EL/PI/product liability insurance — [Sprintlaw, Starting a solar panel business (12 Sep 2025)](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/how-to-start-a-solar-panel-business-in-the-uk-legal-essentials-agreements-and-compliance/)
- Typical UK commercial case-study contents: system size (kWp) and panel count, annual generation (kWh) and specific yield, first-year and 25-year savings, payback, self-sufficiency %, CO2 saved, kit specs, roof/orientation, on-site use vs export — [Sustain Commercial Solar case studies](https://www.sustaincommercialsolar.com/case-studies); [Infinity Energy case studies](https://www.infinity-energy.co.uk/commercial/solar-panels/case-studies/); [HES Renewables case studies](https://hesrenewables.com/commercial-solar/case-studies/) (search summary); example on a new installer's site: "294 kW system, 560 panels, 4.5-year payback, £1.2m savings projection" — [Generate Solar EV](https://generate-solarev.co.uk/)
- Case-study accuracy controls: verify before/after states, "Disclose meaningful changes in lighting, weather, staging", never use generative editing to add completed work, keep scope distinct from other trades — [Nexxen checklist (Canada)](https://nexxensolutions.ca/resources/contractor-project-photos-case-study-permission-checklist)
- PPA funders onboard EPCs through partner portals (UrbanVolt) — a route to funded first projects where the funder holds the client contract — [UrbanVolt](https://urbanvolt.com/guides/how-solar-epcs-unlock-funded-pipeline-with-a-ppa/)

### Inferences
- The founders' own/associated premises (e.g. the electrical business's unit) is the lowest-friction first case study and MCS assessment site, with full photo rights and no permission problems; a family/friend business or a local sports club/church at cost is the second. Community projects also generate local press and Google reviews.
- Continuing as a subcontractor to the existing main contractor (or another MCS-certified installer) but with a written clause granting case-study/photo rights is the fastest way to a *large* commercial reference; it is worth trading margin for that clause.
- O&M, thermal-imaging inspection, EICR/PV periodic verification (BS EN 62446 style) and cleaning contracts produce report-based case studies within weeks and introduce the firm to sites for later repowering/extension work.
- A "pilot PPA" with a funder partner is plausible: funders like UrbanVolt take the client contract and monitoring, so the new firm's role (design + install) is exactly what it can evidence; but funders will require MCS/NICEIC, insurance and warranties, so this sits after MCS in the sequence.

### Gaps
- No documented UK example of a specific new installer's "first three case studies" strategy was found; the routes above are synthesised from MCS requirements, start-up guides and case-study practice.
- Reddit/forum anecdotes were inaccessible.

## Key Question 6 — Examples of new/spin-out firms whose sites handle "new company, experienced team"

### Takeaway
Real examples (mostly UK, one US) show the pattern that works: state the founding year plainly, lead with the team's hands-on experience in specific terms, front-load accreditations and reviews, and (where available) name predecessor experience explicitly rather than implying corporate history. None of the UK examples I found quantify pre-founding MWp — an opportunity for the founders to differentiate with substantiated numbers.

### Cited Findings
1. **Generate Solar EV (Nottingham, UK)** — homepage: "We're not a sales company. We're a team of installers, electricians, and engineers with decades of hands-on experience in solar and electrical work"; "founded on a simple principle: give people clear advice and deliver systems that actually work"; displays NICEIC, RECC, TrustMark, Flexi-Orb, MCS, ISO 9001/14001/45001 logos, a 5.0 Google rating (30+ reviews), a 25-year performance warranty and one detailed commercial case study (RAM Extrusion, 294 kW). Shows how individual "since [year]" bios substitute for corporate history early on.
6. **Experienced Solar (New Mexico, USA; founded 2015)** — non-UK, but the clearest "successor firm" wording: founder ran "Direct Power & Water Corporation (DPW) in New Mexico" for 16 years; "A core group of the same outstanding professionals who made DPW a success work for the new company, Experienced Solar"; "Locally owned since 2015"; licence numbers shown; "Licensed, Bonded, Insured" — [experiencedsolar.net/about](https://www.experiencedsolar.net/about)

This is the incumbent template the founders cannot copy yet and should not imitate with borrowed projects.

### Inferences
- Effective pattern: "[NewCo] was formed in 2026 by [names], electricians who have installed commercial solar PV since 2023 as subcontractors to a national installer" + numbers + accreditations + reviews + one real case study. The JTD formula ("incorporated in 2024, core team brings 10+ years") and the Experienced Solar formula (named predecessor experience) are the two honest templates; the Coys ruling is what happens when a site implies continuity that does not exist.
- The founders can outperform all UK examples above simply by quantifying (MWp, sites, panel counts, roof types) with evidence on file, and by being explicit about the subcontract role.

### Gaps

### Inferences

**(1) Safe-wording guide** (all numbers in brackets must be backed by records held on file)

Use:
1. "[NewCo] was founded in 2026 by brothers [A] and [B], qualified electricians who have installed commercial rooftop solar PV since 2023 as subcontractors to a national solar installer."
2. "Between 2023 and 2026 our founders personally installed more than [X] MWp of commercial PV across [Y] sites — from [roof type] warehouses to [roof type] retail units — working as a subcontract installation team."
3. "That work was delivered under other companies' contracts, so we can't show it as our own. What we can show is exactly how we work: [link to process page], and our first projects as [NewCo] below."
4. "Our founders have hands-on experience with [SolarEdge/Fronius/Huawei] inverter platforms, [K2/Van der Valk/Schletter] mounting systems and G99 grid connections up to [Z] kW." (name only kit they have actually installed)
5. "References from site managers who have supervised our founders' work are available on request." (only once permission is held)
6. "The founders also run Murray Electrical, an NICEIC-registered [confirm] electrical contractor established in [year]; reviews shown here relate to that business."
7. "MCS-certified for solar PV (certificate [number])" — only after certification; until then say nothing about MCS.
8. "Example system: 100 kWp on a [type] roof — modelled figures using [PV*SOL/PVsyst] with stated assumptions; this is an illustration, not an installed project."
9. "Insured: £[5]m public liability, £[n]m professional indemnity, employers' liability — certificates available on request."
10. "[Name], Site Manager at a national solar installer (quoted in a personal capacity): '[verbatim quote]'."

Avoid:
1. "Our portfolio includes [projects done as a subcontractor]" / "Projects we've delivered" — attributes other companies' contracts to the new firm (3.1, 3.3).
2. "Established 20[Murray Electrical year]" or "Over 10 years in business" for the solar company — Coys-type trading-history misrepresentation unless same legal entity.
3. "Trusted by [main contractor's client names]" or client logos from subcontract work — no relationship; likely confidentiality/passing-off exposure.
4. "MCS accredited" / "MCS application in progress" before certification — MCS Agreement cl. 29 and buyer red flag.
5. "Over [X] MW installed" with no role qualifier and no records — unsubstantiated objective claim (3.7) and misleading by omission (3.3).
6. "Enphase Platinum partner" / "Approved installer" for any brand without the actual tier/status letter — 3.52-type unauthorised endorsement claim.

**(2) Prioritised checklist (impact × ease for a new commercial-only firm)**

| Rank | Badge | Why it matters | Ease / time / cost | Show on site? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | NICEIC or NAPIT electrical registration (confirm status of Murray Electrical / NewCo) | Mandatory expectation in every buyer guide; prerequisite for MCS | Likely already held; transfer/extend to NewCo | Yes, day 1 |
| 2 | Insurance: PL £5m (min £2m for MCS), PI, EL, contractors all-risks | Buyers and funders ask for certificates before work starts | Days; broker | Yes, with limits |
| 3 | Level 3 Solar PV award for Technical Supervisor (C&G 2922-34 / EAL / LCL) + EESS if batteries | MCS prerequisite; competence proof | 3–5 days if already electricians | Yes |
| 4 | MCS Solar PV certification (Certsure/NAPIT/IAA/Amtivo/Simply) | "Walk away" criterion for buyers; unlocks IBG products, SEUK affiliate membership | 2–4 months; ~£1,044 first year (NICEIC); needs one compliant ≤50 kWp install | Yes, once held |
| 5 | SSIP badge: CHAS (main contractors) or SafeContractor (FM clients) — pick one | PQQ gate for commercial/industrial sites; evidences CDM/RAMS competence | Days–weeks; £429–£540/yr; needs H&S documentation set | Yes |
| 6 | IPAF, PASMA, CSCS/ECS cards, asbestos awareness | Named in PQQs and buyer guides for roof work | 1–2 days each | Yes (team page) |
| 7 | Insurance-backed workmanship guarantee (QANW/HIES/GGF or MCS-approved product) on every job | "The one that matters most for commercial buyers" | Per job, quote-based; confirm non-domestic availability for a new company | Yes, as a promise |
| 8 | Manufacturer certifications: Enphase (free online), Fronius trained installer (free 1-day), SolaX Silver, Tesla Powerwall (online), Huawei/SolarEdge via distributor | Fast, verifiable, warranty-linked | Hours–days; free/low cost; tiers need volume | Yes, exact status only |
| 9 | Solar Energy UK complementary affiliate membership (needs MCS, turnover <£500k) | Association signal; directory listing | Free once MCS held; check logo rights | Yes if permitted |
| 10 | Constructionline Silver/Gold | Public-sector/tier-1 frameworks | Needs two years' full accounts — year 2 | Later |
| 11 | ISO 9001/14001/45001 | Expected for large (1 MW+) tenders | 6–12 months; ~£2.25–2.75k per standard | Later |
| 12 | RECC/HIES, TrustMark, Which?/Checkatrade | Domestic-consumer signals; not needed for commercial-only | Skip unless adding domestic work | Optional |

**(3) 90-day plan to acquire first case studies** (assumes founders remain able to subcontract in parallel)

- Days 1–14: Read the subcontract (confidentiality, publicity, non-solicit, IP) and send the written permission request to the main contractor for aggregate figures, cleared photos and site-manager references **[solicitor to review]**. Compile the private evidence file behind every number. Bind insurance. Book the Level 3 PV award (if not held) and IPAF/PASMA/CSCS. Register for Enphase University, Fronius E-Academy/FTI day, SolaX and Tesla portals. Start the SSIP (CHAS or SafeContractor) documentation set. Decide the MCS certification body and open the application. Set up the Google Business Profile correctly (edit the existing profile only if NewCo genuinely is the same business; otherwise create a new one). Publish the "interim" website: founder bios with substantiated numbers, process page, sample (redacted) design/handover documents, modelled example-system pages labelled as such, insurance details.
- Days 15–45: Deliver **Case study 1** — a ≤50 kWp system on the founders'/electrical business's own premises or a friendly SME/family business, built to MIS 3002 so it serves as the MCS assessment installation. Photograph before/during/after, record commissioning data (BS EN 62446 verification certificate), G99/G98 paperwork and monitoring set-up. Secure a written case-study consent and a client quote. Pitch **Case study 2** to a community building/sports club/church at cost or a funded pilot via a PPA partner portal (UrbanVolt-type), and negotiate case-study rights in writing in any subcontract work taken during this period.
- Days 46–75: MCS assessment on Case study 1; close non-conformities. Deliver Case study 2 and an **O&M/inspection job (Case study 3)** — thermal-imaging/performance audit or periodic verification on an existing commercial array — which yields a report-based case study and a reference within days. Collect LinkedIn recommendations from main-contractor site managers (personal capacity) and Google reviews from Cases 1–3.
- Days 76–90: Publish three case studies in the situation/complication/solution/outcome format with kWp, panels, kit, modelled vs early actual generation, savings, payback, CO2, quote and photos; add MCS certificate number and any manufacturer certifications gained; apply for Solar Energy UK affiliate membership; replace the "example system" pages' prominence with real projects; approach a PPA funder partner with the now-complete credential set. Re-check every claim on the site against the evidence file before launch.

### Gaps
- The plan assumes the founders are already NICEIC/NAPIT-registered through Murray Electrical and can transfer/extend registration; this was not verifiable. It also assumes the subcontract does not contain an enforceable non-compete covering solar work — a solicitor must confirm before launch.
- Whether IBG providers will cover a new company's first commercial jobs, and exact PI limits demanded by PPA funders, remain unconfirmed.
