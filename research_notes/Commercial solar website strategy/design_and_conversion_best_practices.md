# Commercial solar installer websites: design showcases, conversion and SEO best practice (research notes)

Scope: evidence on what makes a solar installer website effective, drawn from (a) 2024–2026 "best solar websites" roundups and design showcases and (b) solar marketing / web-design agency guidance, weighted toward commercial (B2B) solar and PPA lead generation for a new two-person UK installer with no portfolio yet. Research date: 28 September 2026.

Source-quality note for the writer: almost every roundup found is written by a web-design or marketing agency that sells solar websites (Comrade Web, Hook Agency, Novule, DemandConvert, SolarGenix, 5xBusiness, Zixel, Snapps.ai). Their "praise" is useful as a catalogue of design patterns but is not independent testing. Statistics in these pieces are frequently unsourced or recycled from generic landing-page studies; I flag each such case inline. Nearly all praised sites are US residential; genuinely commercial examples are few and are called out explicitly.

## Key question 2: What page set and information architecture do experts recommend, and what about a separate PPA landing page?

### Takeaway
Experts converge on a small core (Home, service pages split by segment with commercial separate, About/team with credentials, Process, Case studies/portfolio, FAQ, Financing options, Contact/quote) plus location pages for towns genuinely served; nobody explicitly prescribes "a PPA landing page", but the combined evidence (one decision per page, dedicated pages converting far better than homepages, finance buyers needing to compare alternatives, and UK funders like Smart Ease and installers like Solarsense running standalone PPA pages) strongly supports a dedicated PPA/funding page with its own qualification criteria and CTA.

### Cited Findings
- Sunvoy (solar CRM vendor; 6 May 2023, older but principles still cited) says the essential pages are a homepage with a clear value proposition, an About page (typically the "2nd most-visited page"), an FAQ "addressing objections, not just common questions", a Process/timeline page, and a blog only "if maintained; otherwise remove". Its homepage block order: hero video or professional imagery; a single benefit-focused primary CTA; social proof (systems installed, years active, kWh produced); testimonials with real names/photos; certification/association logos; team introductions with photos — [Sunvoy](https://sunvoy.com/blog/solar-marketing-website)
- Snapps.ai (site-builder vendor, 25 Oct 2024) lists essential pages as Home, About Us, Services, Products, FAQ, Contact, Blog, Testimonials, "Financing Options", Gallery — [Snapps.ai](https://www.snapps.ai/best-website-builder-solar-panel-installation-services/)
- A UK guide (5xBusiness, 2025) says must-have pages are one per service (it lists Solar Panel Installation, Battery Storage, Solar + Battery Packages, Inverter Replacement, EV Charger, and "Commercial Solar options") plus "location-specific pages for towns served (e.g., 'Solar Installers in Milton Keynes')", and recommends a homepage with a bold savings headline, plain-language benefits, dual CTAs "Book Free Survey" and "Get a Quote", real installation photography and trust badges — [5xBusiness](https://5xbusiness.co.uk/blog/website-design-for-solar-businesses-how-to-build-a-high-converting-solar-website-uk-guide/)
- UK SEO consultancy Lillian Purge recommends "A page per service you install, meaning panel installation, battery storage plus commercial work separately rather than as a list", and "Area pages for the towns you genuinely cover, describing real work done there rather than a template with a place name swapped in" — [Lillian Purge](https://www.lillianpurge.co.uk/the-complete-seo-guide-for-solar-companies-in-the-uk)
- Novule's installer-specific feature list: an installation-credentials section (MCS, NAPIT, team experience), a step-by-step installation process, a real project portfolio, a warranty/guarantee clarity page, a system-monitoring section, a local service-area definition, and team qualifications; it also recommends "service area filters to reduce unqualified inquiries" — [Novule: Solar Panel Website Design](https://www.novule.com/blog/solar-panel-website-design)
- Target ICT (UK) lists intuitive navigation, detailed service descriptions with a portfolio of past projects, educational resources (blogs, FAQs, downloadable guides), high-quality installation imagery/video, prominent CTAs with online scheduling/quoting, and SEO — [Target ICT](https://targetict.co.uk/blog/must-have-features-of-a-solar-installer-website.html)
- Comrade's principles include "clear navigation for residential vs. commercial paths" — [Comrade Web](https://comradeweb.com/blog/best-solar-websites/); SolarGenix praises SunShare's "targeted navigation for residents, businesses, partners" and TRC's "Markets" vs "Services" split — [SolarGenix](https://solargenix.ai/blog/best-solar-website-designs)
- SurgePV's commercial-marketing guidance (30 Aug 2026) says essential content for facility and finance audiences includes data-readiness checklists (which meter, tariff and operating records a preliminary assessment needs), "question maps" of roof geometry/shade/electrical context rather than finished designs, assumption registers behind any scenario comparison, facility-operations reviews (access, staging, safety, handover), and "role-specific review packs" that separate finance materials from facility materials — [SurgePV: Commercial Solar Marketing](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/commercial-solar-marketing)
- SurgePV's conversion checklist says to "give each high-intent page one reader decision" ("If the page asks for a demo, quote, consultation, download, newsletter signup, and phone call with equal weight, the visitor has to infer the intended path"), to "match the opening promise to the acquisition source" (a search about commercial roof assessment should not land on residential savings copy), to "state who the service is for and where it applies" (customer type, project type, market boundary, exclusions), and to "build a deliberate next-page path" — [SurgePV: 20 changes](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/solar-website-conversion)
- Web Tonic's 2026 solar landing-page statistics (secondary compilation) state "Dedicated landing pages achieve 5× higher conversion than generic homepages for solar campaigns" and "Removing top navigation increases landing page conversions by 10–25%" (attributed to Ping Tree Systems) — [Web Tonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/solar-energy-landing-page-statistics)
- SurgePV's role-specific messaging says finance teams need "decision alternatives clearly presented (not just one option)" and to see "what decision is being requested and which inputs make the scenario move" — [SurgePV: role-specific messaging](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/how-to-create-role-specific-solar-messaging-for-cfos-facilities-and-sustainability-teams)
- UK PPA example page structure (Smart Ease, a UK funder): headline "Would a solar PPA suit your business?"; (1) definition — "a long-term electricity supply agreement" where the provider installs, owns and maintains the system and sells the electricity at reduced rates; (2) four benefits — no upfront capital, long-term bill savings, expert maintenance, trackable carbon reductions; (3) ideal profile — systems over 70 kW, monthly bills of £2,000+, daytime-heavy consistent loads, preference for outsourced maintenance, contract "7−30 years", examples such as shopping centres, schools, cold storage, distribution centres, supermarkets, agriculture; (4) who it does not suit — night-operating businesses, irregular production runs, seasonal enterprises; a case study ("Cloudy Bay's solar PPA success story"); CTAs "Get in touch" and "Schedule a call". Gaps on that page: no comparison with outright purchase/lease and no end-of-term provisions — [Smart Ease](https://smartease.uk/solar-ppa-for-business/)
- SEIA's PPA explainer (the definition most sites paraphrase) describes a PPA as an arrangement where the developer handles design, permitting, financing and installation "at little to no cost", sells power at a fixed rate "typically lower than the local utility's retail rate", terms run 10–25 years with the developer responsible for O&M, and at end of term the customer can extend, have the system removed, or buy it — [SEIA](https://seia.org/research-resources/solar-power-purchase-agreements/)
- Financing-comparison pages are a common pattern: PG&E's financing page offers a comparison table of buying, leasing and PPA — [PG&E](https://www.pge.com/en/clean-energy/solar/getting-started-with-solar/financing-options-for-solar.html); NuWatt publishes "Cash vs Loan vs Lease vs PPA" comparisons — [NuWatt](https://nuwattenergy.com/en/solar-financing-2026); BAESS offers a side-by-side calculator of 25-year net benefit, payback and monthly payment across the four routes — [BAESS](https://www.baess.app/tools/solar-financing-calculator)
- Search results also surfaced a UK commercial installer (Solarsense) that maintains a dedicated "Solar Power Purchase Agreement" page under its commercial section, and Zixel lists Solarsense as a design example — [Solarsense PPA page](https://solarsense-uk.com/commercial-renewable-energy-for-businesses/solar-power-purchase-agreement/); [Zixel](https://www.zixel.co.uk/web-design-examples/solar-website-examples/)

### Inferences
- A separate PPA/funded-solar page is justified by four cited threads: one decision per page (SurgePV), dedicated pages out-converting homepages (Web Tonic), finance buyers wanting alternatives laid out side by side (SurgePV, PG&E-style tables), and UK funders/installers already treating PPA as a standalone page with explicit eligibility thresholds (Smart Ease). The eligibility block ("who this suits / who it doesn't") is the single most useful thing to copy from Smart Ease because it pre-qualifies leads before they submit.
- For a company with no portfolio, the SurgePV pattern of publishing "question maps", data-readiness checklists and assumption registers is a credible substitute for case studies: it demonstrates competence through process transparency rather than past projects.
- Because the company sells both capital-purchase installs and funded PPAs, the IA should give each its own page and cross-link them via a comparison table (capex vs PPA vs lease/loan) so a finance director can see the alternatives on one screen.

### Gaps
- No source explicitly states "commercial installers should build a separate PPA landing page"; the recommendation above is an inference from adjacent evidence.
- No expert guidance found on how a PPA page should present funder relationships (e.g. naming funding partners vs white-labelling) — Smart Ease is itself the funder.

## Key question 3: Which conversion elements matter most for B2B solar leads, and what form length is optimal?

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced levers are: a specific, audience-matched headline; a short (3–4 field) or staged multi-step form placed high on the page; a savings/ROI calculator that shows its assumptions; benefit-worded CTAs ("Book a free site survey", not "Submit"); trust badges next to the CTA; a numbered process; and fast, mobile-first pages with human follow-up within minutes — with commercial buyers specifically needing decision alternatives, sourced assumptions and role-specific content rather than "cut your bill" claims.

### Cited Findings

**Benchmarks and what moves the needle**
- Solar landing pages "convert at just 1–2%, while top performers hit 6–9%" (Web Tonic citing Get Green Leads 2026); energy-sector median 2.9% / top performers 6.5% (citing CuFinder); "Headline, form position, and field count account for 60–70% of conversion lift" (attributed to SurgePV 2026); benefit-driven CTAs such as "Get My Free Solar Savings Estimate" outperform generic ones "by 15–25%" — [Web Tonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/solar-energy-landing-page-statistics)
- An Apexure solar landing-page case study reports 8.36% conversion — [Apexure](https://www.apexure.com/case-study/solar-landing-page-case-study/)
- Callpage (4 Feb 2025): solar companies' conversion rates run "between 13% and 17%" (lead-to-sale, source not detailed); "78% of solar customers choose the first company that responds to their inquiry"; "55% of companies take five or more days to respond"; referral programmes convert "30% better than other lead sources" — [Callpage](https://www.callpage.io/blog/posts/strategies-for-solar-lead-generation)

**Hero messaging**
- Hook praises "customer-focused headlines" over company accomplishments; DemandConvert praises SelectEnergy's "targeted H1/H2 for commercial ROI" and Blue Raven's "immediate financial specifics above fold" — [Hook Agency](https://hookagency.com/blog/solar-website-design-examples/); [DemandConvert](https://demandconvert.com/learn/blog/solar-website-design-examples/)
- SurgePV: "Replace generic benefits with decision tasks" — "Save time and money" is unverifiable; name concrete tasks such as "comparing project scenarios" or "preparing a site record"; and state customer type, project type and exclusions so unsuitable visitors leave before submitting — [SurgePV: 20 changes](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/solar-website-conversion)
- UK agency Mouthy Marketing: use specific, quantifiable claims ("We saved £428 in our first year" rather than vague percentages) and tailor headlines to motivation (cost, security, climate) — [Mouthy Marketing](https://www.mouthymarketing.co.uk/marketing-for-solar-companies-is-broken-heres-how-to-fix-it/)

**Savings/ROI calculators and bill/roof inputs**
- Novule praises Tesla's "Know how much you could save" calculator and Palmetto's instant-estimate cost calculator; Comrade praises REC's "Calculate My Savings" secondary CTA; Hook/DemandConvert praise ProHauz's ROI calculator — [Novule](https://www.novule.com/blog/best-solar-company-websites); [Comrade Web](https://comradeweb.com/blog/best-solar-websites/)
- ConvertSite/ConvertCalculator (2025) on commercial leads: "Most potential customers aren't ready to 'talk to sales' right away. They want to know one thing first: What's in it for me?"; recommended calculator inputs are property type and location, energy usage and costs, roof square footage, battery needs and installation timeline, with outputs of estimated savings, ROI or payback; add instant qualification, consultation scheduling from the calculator and automated follow-up; one case reports "Leads increased by 2.4x" in 30 days with 62% open rates on auto follow-ups (vendor case) — [ConvertSite](https://www.convertsite.com/blog/commercial-solar-leads)
- The same vendor's search snippet claims "Commercial solar calculators maintain a 25-35% conversion advantage over basic forms" (seen only in the search summary; not verified on the fetched page; vendor claim) — [ConvertSite](https://www.convertsite.com/blog/commercial-solar-leads)
- Callpage: "ROI calculators with real installation examples" and gating calculators for an email address — [Callpage](https://www.callpage.io/blog/posts/strategies-for-solar-lead-generation)

- Lillian Purge (UK): "Do not mark up savings figures, payback periods or performance claims as structured facts" and present payback periods as illustrations, not facts — [Lillian Purge](https://www.lillianpurge.co.uk/the-complete-seo-guide-for-solar-companies-in-the-uk)

**Form length and staging**
- Comrade: "Short forms (3–4 fields maximum)" — [Comrade Web](https://comradeweb.com/blog/best-solar-websites/)
- Web Tonic: "Reducing form fields from 7 to 3 increased form completion by 145%" and cut cost per lead from €48 to €19 (Forte Solar case via Grow Conversions); "Optimal fields: name, phone, postcode, and optionally monthly electricity spend" — [Web Tonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/solar-energy-landing-page-statistics)

- SurgePV: finance teams need alternatives, "transparent assumptions and their sources", sensitivity analysis and the accounting/tax/lender review pathway; facility managers need site access, disruption planning, roof/ground conditions, safety and maintenance responsibilities; "one 'cut your electricity bill' campaign does not serve both jobs" — [SurgePV: Commercial Solar Marketing](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/commercial-solar-marketing)

### Inferences
- For a B2B audience the evidence points to a *two-tier* capture: a very short first-touch form (company, name, phone/email, postcode, approximate monthly bill or annual kWh) and an optional second stage (half-hourly data / bill upload, roof area, site constraints) — matching SurgePV's "stage sensitive inputs" and the involve.me finding that qualification dropdowns cost some conversion but raise lead quality. Given the value of a commercial lead, trading a few points of conversion for a bill or consumption figure is a reasonable choice, but keep it optional.
- "Book a free site survey" or "Request a feasibility check" is a better primary CTA than "Get a free quote" for UK commercial buyers, because Lillian Purge reports "free quote" reads as a sales trap and SurgePV wants the CTA to describe the actual next step (a survey is what actually happens next in commercial solar).
- Autoplay hero video is a genuine trade-off: praised for polish in roundups, penalised for Core Web Vitals in the more technical analyses. A short, click-to-play or lazy-loaded clip is the safe middle ground.
- Speed-to-lead (minutes, not days) is the highest-leverage operational element and is essentially free for a two-person firm; a callback/booking widget matters more than live chat, which a two-person team cannot staff.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found that isolates optimal form length specifically for *commercial* solar leads; the numeric form-field data are residential or cross-industry.
- No independent (non-vendor) evidence found for live chat or chatbots lifting solar conversions.
- No source quantified the impact of "book a site survey" versus "get a quote" wording.

## Key question 4: What do experts say about photography, visual identity and tone for commercial buyers?

### Takeaway
Every design source agrees that real, specific project photography (wide roof shots, close-ups of workmanship, team at work, drone/aerial where available) beats stock and AI imagery, with UK critics adding that even real drone-roof shots fail when they show "no context, no people, no emotion"; for commercial buyers the tone should be analytical and transparent about assumptions, role-specific (finance vs facilities vs sustainability), in plain "you"-focused language, and must never present preliminary designs or public roof images as completed projects.

### Inferences
- For a company with no installs yet, the honest visual strategy is: (a) real photos of the two founders on site, with tools and PPE, in recognisable UK commercial settings (photographed at a partner's or previous-employer project only with permission and clear labelling), (b) clearly labelled "illustrative design" renders/roof layouts rather than anything that could be read as a completed job, and (c) partner/manufacturer imagery credited as such. SurgePV's warning about presenting preliminary designs as accepted projects applies directly.
- Drone imagery is praised in US roundups but is exactly the "roof with no people" pattern Mouthy Marketing criticises; the reconciliation is to use aerials for scale and context while pairing them with people-on-site and interior/operational shots.
- Tone for this audience should lean toward the engineering-transparency register (assumptions, sources, survey process) rather than the residential "save £££ / go green" register; the residential "barbecue language" advice still applies to sentence-level plainness.

### Gaps
- No expert source found that discusses 3D renders or CGI for solar websites specifically; the only relevant guidance is SurgePV's warning about preliminary designs.
- No UK-specific source on commercial solar brand identity (colour, type) beyond generic colour-meaning claims; the createtoday.io statistics are gallery-wide, not commercial-specific.
- Corbae Creative's "Misguided solar stock photos" piece could not be fetched.

- Mouthy Marketing (UK): "technical specs, generic messaging, and way too many rooftop stock images"; assuming every buyer is climate-motivated; roof shots with "No context. No people. No emotion."; ads that "burn budget with vague ads, generic keywords, and poorly optimised landing pages" — [Mouthy Marketing](https://www.mouthymarketing.co.uk/marketing-for-solar-companies-is-broken-heres-how-to-fix-it/)
- Novule: positioning as "energy consultants, financial advisors, and environmental activists all at once" — [Novule: Solar Panel Website Design](https://www.novule.com/blog/solar-panel-website-design)
- Diamond Group: automated follow-up sequences with 48-hour delays; credentials buried in fine print; generic stock photography — [Diamond Group](https://www.diamond-group.co/blog/how-solar-companies-build-trust-before-the-first-call)
- Creative Nurds (UK/EU solar web agency; page is a gated lead magnet so only the framing is visible): "Most websites are difficult to navigate and confusing to understand", stock photos/generic templates are "the most common error", and "Mobile website design usually still comes as an afterthought" — [Creative Nurds](https://www.creativenurds.com/solar-website-mistakes)
- Greentech Renewables: unclear phone visibility, too-narrow geographic focus, insufficient keyword integration, thin content — [Greentech Renewables](https://www.greentechrenewables.com/article/solar-leads-mastering-landing-page)
- Web Tonic: the "three conversion killers" are weak value propositions, excessive form fields and slow page speed — [Web Tonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/solar-energy-landing-page-statistics)

### Inferences
- Several "mistakes" are direct contradictions of things the roundups praise (autoplay/drone video, many CTAs); the resolution is that these devices are praised when a large brand can afford the performance cost and penalised for small installers whose sites already average 45/100 on PageSpeed.
- For a new firm the cheapest mistakes to avoid are behavioural: slow follow-up, unlabelled or borrowed imagery, and unsourced savings claims.

## Key question 7: Which platforms do solar agencies recommend for small installers, and why?

### Takeaway
There is no solar-specific consensus: UK solar web guides default to WordPress, a Squarespace-specialist agency recommends Squarespace, builder comparisons rate Webflow highest for SEO/customisation but hardest for non-developers, and Wix is criticised for mobile performance; the more technical solar analyses warn that off-the-shelf WordPress themes add speed-killing bloat, so the choice hinges on who will maintain the site and how much custom form/calculator work is needed.

### Cited Findings
- Snapps.ai builder comparison (25 Oct 2024; vendor ranks itself first): Squarespace — "sleek, modern designs perfect for showcasing solar panel installations", scheduling and portfolio built in, but "absence of phone support"; Webflow — strong "SEO Optimization", extensive customisation, CRM integration, real-time form notifications and file upload, but "quite complicated compared to other website builders" for non-developers; Wix — appointment booking, Wix Chat and analytics, but a "buggy" editor with "poor" mobile load times; 10Web — AI builder that users report "caused numerous errors" — [Snapps.ai](https://www.snapps.ai/best-website-builder-solar-panel-installation-services/)
- 5xBusiness (UK solar web guide): WordPress is the emphasised platform — [5xBusiness](https://5xbusiness.co.uk/blog/website-design-for-solar-businesses-how-to-build-a-high-converting-solar-website-uk-guide/)
- Novule (Squarespace-specialist agency) builds solar sites on Squarespace — [Novule: Solar Panel Website Design](https://www.novule.com/blog/solar-panel-website-design)
- DemandConvert warns that "Commercial WordPress themes inject bloat reducing speed" — [DemandConvert](https://demandconvert.com/learn/blog/solar-website-design-examples/)
- Hook Agency specifies no platform but insists sites be "super easy for you to edit on the backend" — [Hook Agency: Solar Website Design](https://hookagency.com/solar-marketing/website-design/)
- General (non-solar) comparisons: Webflow "wins for design-led businesses that want speed and low upkeep, while WordPress wins for content-heavy sites, big stores, and teams that want full ownership"; Webflow includes hosting and security, WordPress needs separately managed hosting — [The Alien Design](https://www.thealien.design/insights/webflow-vs-wordpress); [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/website/webflow-vs-wordpress)
- Framer: a free "Solarify" solar/energy Framer template (12 inner pages, 20+ sections) is listed on Awwwards Market — [Awwwards Market](https://www.awwwards.com/market/product_6811c4648572a876121981)
- Sunvoy is platform-agnostic but recommends system fonts, HTTPS with 301s, and Google Analytics plus Hotjar heatmaps reviewed monthly — [Sunvoy](https://sunvoy.com/blog/solar-marketing-website)

### Inferences
- For a two-person firm that wants speed, low maintenance and a custom PPA calculator/multi-step form, Webflow (or Framer) with an embedded form/calculator tool is the closest fit to the cited criteria; WordPress remains the safe UK-agency default if a local developer will maintain it, provided a lightweight theme is used; Squarespace/Wix trade flexibility for ease and are weaker for custom qualification flows.
- HubSpot CMS was not mentioned by any solar-specific source; its case would rest on CRM/speed-to-lead integration rather than design.

### Gaps
- No solar marketing agency source found that recommends Framer or HubSpot CMS specifically.
- No performance benchmark found comparing solar sites by platform.

## Recommended site outline and prioritised conversion/trust checklist (synthesis)

### Takeaway
The outline below combines the consensus page set (KQ2), the highest-evidence conversion elements (KQ3), UK trust signals (KQ5) and the commercial-buyer content patterns (KQ4) into a page-by-page structure that a new UK commercial installer with a PPA offer can build without a portfolio; the checklist rates each element by strength of evidence and fit for a two-person firm.

### Cited Findings
- Block ordering draws on Sunvoy's homepage sequence (hero, single CTA, social proof, testimonials, certifications, team) — [Sunvoy](https://sunvoy.com/blog/solar-marketing-website); 5xBusiness's dual CTAs and trust badges — [5xBusiness](https://5xbusiness.co.uk/blog/website-design-for-solar-businesses-how-to-build-a-high-converting-solar-website-uk-guide/); SurgePV's one-decision-per-page, audience statement, evidence-beside-claim and process-transparency rules — [SurgePV: 20 changes](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/solar-website-conversion); Smart Ease's PPA page structure — [Smart Ease](https://smartease.uk/solar-ppa-for-business/); Lillian Purge's service/area/schema rules — [Lillian Purge](https://www.lillianpurge.co.uk/the-complete-seo-guide-for-solar-companies-in-the-uk); the UK commercial accreditation stack — [Solar Panels for Businesses](https://solarpanelsforbusinesses.co.uk/accreditations/); form-length evidence — [Web Tonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/solar-energy-landing-page-statistics); [involve.me](https://www.involve.me/blog/landing-page-statistics); speed-to-lead — [Callpage](https://www.callpage.io/blog/posts/strategies-for-solar-lead-generation); [Diamond Group](https://www.diamond-group.co/blog/how-solar-companies-build-trust-before-the-first-call).

### Inferences

**(1) Recommended page-by-page outline (blocks in order)**

Global elements (every page)
1. Header: logo; nav = Commercial Solar | Solar PPA (Funded Solar) | Sectors | How It Works | About | Resources | Contact; clickable phone number; sticky primary CTA "Book a free site survey" (Comrade sticky CTA; Greentech/Sunvoy phone prominence; 5xBusiness "Book Free Survey").
2. Footer: NAP, opening hours, areas served (linked to area pages), accreditation logos, review-platform links, privacy/complaints policy, LocalBusiness schema (DemandConvert NAP footer; Lillian Purge schema).

Home
1. Hero: audience-specific headline naming who it is for and where ("Commercial solar and funded PPAs for [region] businesses paying £X,000+/month for electricity"), one-line sub-head on outcome, primary CTA "Book a free site survey", secondary CTA "Check PPA eligibility"; real photo of founders on a commercial roof (SurgePV state-who-it's-for; 5xBusiness dual CTAs; Hook customer-focused headline).
2. Trust bar directly under hero: MCS, NICEIC/NAPIT, SSIP (CHAS/SafeContractor), insurance-backed warranty provider, Solar Energy UK, funder partner logos (Web Tonic badges near CTA; Solar Panels for Businesses trust stack).
3. Two-path chooser: "Buy outright" vs "Zero-capex PPA" cards linking to their pages (Comrade residential/commercial pathing; SunShare segmented nav).
4. "Who we help" sector cards (manufacturing, warehousing/logistics, agriculture, cold storage, retail, schools/public sector) — each linking to a sector page or anchor (Smart Ease ideal-profile list).
5. Numbered process 1-2-3-4-5: enquiry → half-hourly data & bill review → site survey → design & funding options → install & handover/monitoring (Hook/SunBadger 1-2-3; SurgePV publish the process).
6. Founders block: names, photos, qualifications, years in the trade, why two-person means direct accountability (Sunvoy team intros; Saguaro "in-house team creates accountability").
7. Evidence block (substitute for case studies until first jobs): "What we check before we quote" — data-readiness checklist and worked illustrative example labelled "modelled, illustrative" with assumptions listed (SurgePV question maps/assumption registers; Lillian Purge payback as illustration).
8. FAQ (5–7 objection-led questions: roof suitability, disruption, DNO/G99, what happens at end of PPA, who maintains) with FAQPage schema (Sunvoy objection FAQ; Lillian Purge FAQ schema).
9. Closing CTA band with the same two CTAs and a short form (company, name, phone, email, postcode, approx. monthly electricity spend — optional) (Web Tonic optimal fields; involve.me qualification trade-off).

Commercial Solar (service page — capital purchase)
1. Hero with headline on ROI/payback framing, CTA "Book a free site survey".
2. Who it suits / who it doesn't (consumption threshold, daytime load, roof type, tenure) (Smart Ease eligibility pattern; SurgePV exclusions).
3. What you get: design, DNO/G99 application, structural survey, install, commissioning, O&M/monitoring, warranties (Novule warranty clarity; commercialsolarpanelsinstallation.co.uk scope).
4. Savings & ROI section with calculator or illustrative example that exposes assumptions (tariff, degradation, export) and states "modelled, not guaranteed" (SurgePV assumption visibility; Lillian Purge).
5. Finance-director block: comparison table capex vs PPA vs lease/loan (PG&E/NuWatt pattern; SurgePV alternatives).
6. Facilities-manager block: access, work windows, disruption plan, safety, maintenance responsibility (SurgePV facilities guidance).
7. Accreditations & warranties strip; FAQ; CTA band.

Solar PPA / Funded Solar (dedicated landing page)
1. Hero: "Solar power for your business with no capital outlay" + qualification CTA "Check if a PPA suits your site" (Smart Ease headline; Web Tonic dedicated page).
2. How a PPA works in 4 steps (funder pays, we install, you buy the power at a fixed lower rate, funder maintains) (SEIA; Smart Ease definition).
3. Is a PPA right for you: thresholds (e.g. system size, monthly bill, daytime load, lease/freehold), sectors that fit, and who it does not suit (Smart Ease).
4. Term, price escalation, end-of-term options (extend, buy, remove) — stated plainly with "varies by funder" (SEIA; Smart Ease gap noted).
5. PPA vs buying outright vs lease comparison table (SurgePV alternatives; PG&E pattern).
6. Funding partners: logos and how the partnership works (with permission) — the trust substitute for a portfolio.
7. Finance-director FAQ (balance-sheet treatment, credit checks, what happens if we move/sell, roof lease) written as "documented / stated / modelled" where relevant (SurgePV labels).
8. Short eligibility form (company, postcode, monthly electricity spend band, roof ownership, contact) → confirmation page that says who reviews it and when they'll call (SurgePV confirmation job).

Sectors (one page or one per sector as capacity allows)
1. Sector-specific load profile and why solar fits; typical roof/ground options; illustrative system size band; sector FAQs; CTA (Lillian Purge specificity; Smart Ease sector examples).

How It Works / Process
1. Full timeline from enquiry to commissioning with what the client provides at each step (bills, half-hourly data, roof drawings, landlord consent), who reviews, and typical durations (Sunvoy process page; SurgePV data-readiness).

About
1. Founders' story and credentials; certifications with certificate numbers; insurance; partners; values stated as commitments (e.g. "we will tell you if solar doesn't stack up") (Sunvoy About is 2nd most visited; Lillian Purge "say what you would not recommend").

Resources / Guides
1. 4–8 specific, dated guides: commercial solar cost per kWp in [region] 2026; PPA vs capex for SMEs; DNO/G99 explained; roof suitability checklist; half-hourly data guide; grants/funding (captures the "funding" cluster) (Lillian Purge; Martal content topics).

Area pages (3–6 towns genuinely served)
1. Town-specific intro with real local detail (industrial estates, typical roof stock, DNO region), services offered there, drive-time coverage, CTA; no template swaps (Lillian Purge; Mouthy Marketing).

Contact / Book a Survey
1. Short form, phone, email, response-time promise ("we call back within one working hour"), map/service area, what to have ready (Callpage speed-to-lead; Diamond Group).

Case studies (add as soon as the first job completes)
1. Per project: site type, kWp, annual generation (measured vs modelled labelled), funding route, photos with date and location, client quote (Novule portfolio details; SurgePV measured vs modelled; Diamond Group).

**(2) Conversion and trust checklist with priority (High = strong multi-source evidence and feasible now; Medium = supported but lower evidence or later; Low = nice-to-have or contested)**

High priority
- Audience-specific hero headline stating who/where/what outcome (SurgePV; Hook; DemandConvert).
- Primary CTA "Book a free site survey" (or "Request a feasibility check") repeated in header, hero, mid-page and footer; avoid bare "Get a free quote"/"Submit" (5xBusiness; Lillian Purge; SurgePV; SolarGenix).
- First-touch form of 3–5 fields, optional monthly-spend band; staged second step for bill/half-hourly upload (Web Tonic; Comrade; involve.me; SurgePV).
- Human call-back within minutes/one working hour, stated on the site (Callpage 78% first-responder; Diamond Group).
- Trust bar of UK commercial accreditations next to CTAs: MCS, NICEIC/NAPIT, SSIP, insurance-backed warranty, Solar Energy UK, funder logos (Solar Panels for Businesses; Web Tonic; Diamond Group).
- Dedicated PPA page with eligibility criteria and who-it-doesn't-suit (Smart Ease; Web Tonic dedicated pages; SurgePV one decision per page).
- Capex vs PPA vs lease comparison table (SurgePV alternatives; PG&E/NuWatt pattern).
- Numbered process block and full process page (Hook; Sunvoy; SurgePV).
- Real photography of founders on site; no stock or AI panels; label any renders as illustrative (Comrade; SolarGenix; Novule; SurgePV).
- Mobile-first build, PageSpeed 70+ target, no autoplay hero video, lightweight theme/platform (Web Tonic; DemandConvert; SolarGenix).
- Google Business Profile complete with service areas; LocalBusiness/Service/FAQPage schema; no savings figures in schema (Lillian Purge; Mouthy Marketing).
- Google reviews first, then Trustpilot/Checkatrade, with a review request within 48 hours of each completed job (Sunsave criteria; Diamond Group).

Medium priority
- Savings/ROI calculator that exposes assumptions and labels outputs as modelled (Novule; ConvertSite; SurgePV; Lillian Purge).
- Objection-led FAQ on every service page with schema (Sunvoy; Lillian Purge).
- Sector pages and 3–6 genuine area pages (Lillian Purge; 5xBusiness).
- Role-specific content blocks (finance vs facilities vs sustainability) or downloadable "review packs" (SurgePV; Clean Power Marketing).
- Data-readiness checklist / "what we need from you" page as a competence signal in lieu of case studies (SurgePV).
- Callback/booking widget (Calendly-style) rather than staffed live chat for a two-person team (Callpage; live-chat evidence is vendor-only).
- Short, click-to-play founder/explainer video rather than background video (Comrade/Hook praise video; DemandConvert/SolarGenix penalise autoplay).
- Dated, specific guides for the "funding/grants" and "cost" demand clusters (Lillian Purge keyword data).

Low priority / contested
- Animation, cursor effects, big-image transitions of the Awwwards kind (Volta Solar) — visual appeal but performance cost (DemandConvert).
- Live chat/chatbot — only vendor evidence; not staffable by two people.
- Which? Trusted Traders / Checkatrade badges on commercial pages — consumer-facing; secondary to NICEIC/SSIP/ISO for B2B (Sunsave vs Solar Panels for Businesses).
- Impact counters (panels installed, CO₂ saved) — praised in roundups (Hannah Solar, EDF) but not credible until real installs exist; use funder/partner aggregate figures only with permission.
- Exit-intent pop-ups and lead magnets (Sunvoy) — residential tactic; low fit for B2B tone.

### Gaps
- The outline is a synthesis; no single source validates it end-to-end, and none of the cited conversion statistics were measured on UK commercial solar sites specifically.
- Real-world block structures of the praised UK commercial sites (Solarsense, Solar For Business, DB Energy) could not be extracted because the pages did not render in the fetch tool; the writer may want to open them manually to compare against this outline.
