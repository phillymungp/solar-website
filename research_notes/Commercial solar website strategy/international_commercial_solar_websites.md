# International (non-UK) commercial solar & PPA websites — design and structure models

Scope: US, Australia, Ireland, Canada and English-language European commercial (C&I) solar installer / PPA sites, evaluated as structure-and-look models for a new two-person UK commercial solar installer (electricians, 3+ years' subcontract experience, no own portfolio yet). Every site below was fetched live on **2026-09-28** (homepage plus, where one exists, the PPA/financing and process pages). Platform identification comes from the raw HTML of each page (meta generator tags, CDN paths, builder CSS/JS signatures) pulled with curl on the same date; visual-style notes are inferred from the extracted page content, not from rendered screenshots (see Gaps).

## Key Question 1: Which international commercial solar sites explain PPAs most clearly for business owners (vs cash purchase, loan, lease)?

### Takeaway
The clearest explanations pair a one-sentence plain-English definition with (a) a side-by-side comparison of PPA vs lease/loan vs purchase and (b) at least a few hard numbers (term, discount vs grid, who maintains, what happens at end of term). Only a handful of the 26 sites fetched do all three: Solar X (Canada), Todae Solar (Australia), SunPeak (US), AEI Commercial (Ireland) and Select Electrical (Australia); most others give a definition and benefits bullets but no numbers or comparison.

### Cited Findings

- **Todae Solar (Australia)** is the only pure-EPC site with a true PPA | Lease | Purchase comparison table (rows: Upfront Costs, Ownership, Tax Benefits, Operations & Maintenance, Term, Ongoing Costs, Eligible for Rebates, Insurance; PPA column: "No Upfront Costs", "Only pay for the solar energy used", "10–20 years", "All O&M & insurance included"); its definition reads "A Solar PPA allows you to install solar power without any upfront capital investment and simply receive a locked in electricity tariff that provides a discount on your current day grid tariffs." ### Inferences
- Regional vocabulary differs: Australian installers sell PPAs as one of three "finance cards" (PPA / Lease / Purchase); Irish firms wrap PPAs in "Funded Solar" or "Solar as a Service" language and lean on a named funder (SSE, €30m fund, DCC parent); US firms use "customer-owned vs investor-owned" and add ESA/SSA/operating-lease variants. For a UK audience the Irish framing (no capex, fixed price, "we maintain it", gifted at end) is the most transferable.
- The single most persuasive ingredient is concrete numbers a business owner can sanity-check (AEI's €30k minimum spend / 15–20 yrs / 5–20% cheaper; Select's 7–30 yrs / up to 25% / ownership transfer; Solar X's worked 250 kW loan-vs-PPA). Sites that only list benefits (ENVIRIA, Pinergy, Fort, Elite Vin) read as vague even when the design is good.
- A comparison table is rare enough (Todae, SunPeak, Solar X, Activ8-partial) that including one would already put a new UK site ahead of most international peers.

### Gaps
- No site fetched publishes an actual PPA price per kWh or escalator %; Todae's own page contradicts itself on term (5–10 vs 10–20 years) and on insurance ("All O&M & insurance included" vs "customer is responsible for insurance"), so which is current could not be verified.
- VCT Group's dedicated /financing-solar page could not be read (Squarespace page returned mostly CSS to the fetcher; only the H1 "Solar Financing in Canada" was captured), so its comparison, if any, is unknown.
- Thomas Electrical (AU), Greentricity (IE) and Sun Arc Solar (IE) PPA pages were bot-blocked or JavaScript-only and could not be evaluated.

## Key Question 2: Which have the best overall design, and which smaller regional installers have sites that look as good as the national players?

### Takeaway
Judged from structure, copy discipline and build quality (not from rendered screenshots), the most polished C&I sites are the Webflow builds (Onyx Renewables, ForeFront Power, Freedom Power's commercial page, SolarKal) and the headless builds (ENVIRIA, Nexamp), with HubSpot-CMS sites Smart Commercial Energy and SunPeak close behind; among genuinely small or regional installers, SunPeak (Wisconsin), Revel Energy (California), VCT Group (Ontario), Select Electrical (Canberra), Veep Energy (Dublin) and Fort Energy (Galway) present as credibly as the national players by leaning on process, licences, affiliations and a few metric-led projects rather than MW counts.

### Inferences
- "Looks national" correlates less with platform than with three habits: a benefit-led hero sentence, one restrained palette (navy/blue + one accent), and metric-led project cards (kW, kWh/yr, % saved) rather than photo galleries. Fort Energy on Wix and VCT on Squarespace both achieve this.
- Zero-rendering counters are the most common self-inflicted credibility wound; a new site should either hard-code numbers or avoid counters entirely.
- Big developer sites (ForeFront, Sunrock, Solar Landscape, Pivot) look best precisely because of assets a two-person firm cannot copy (drone footage of 5 MW roofs, Fortune-500 logo walls, $140M savings claims), so they are inspiration for tone only.

### Gaps

## Key Question 3: What page structures and conversion patterns (savings calculators, bill-upload quote forms, sector landing pages, ROI examples) recur on the best sites?

### Takeaway
The best C&I sites converge on: a "book a free feasibility / energy analysis" primary CTA (not "get a quote"); a three-way finance section (PPA / lease-or-loan / purchase); sector landing pages (6–16); a numbered process page (4–9 steps); metric-led project cards; and trust blocks built from licences, ISO/CEC-type accreditations, funder names and review scores. Calculators exist but are rare and mostly gated; no fetched form asked for a bill upload (the closest are Activ8's MPRN field and Solar X's monthly-bill slider).

### Inferences
- A sensible hybrid for the UK firm: Select Electrical's PPA page content + SunPeak's navigation and process page + Fort Energy's accreditation-and-outcomes trust block + AEI's three finance routes + Todae's system-size package page (rewritten for 50 kW / 100 kW / 250 kW UK systems) — none of which requires a project portfolio.

- No comparable UK-adjacent pricing (p/kWh, escalator) was found on any international site, so the numbers a UK PPA page should carry must come from the UK researcher or the firm's funder.
