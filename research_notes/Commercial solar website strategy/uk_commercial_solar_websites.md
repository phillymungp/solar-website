Scope: UK companies and UK-facing sites only. All homepages and deep pages below were fetched with WebFetch on **2026-09-28** (raw HTML also pulled with curl the same day for platform detection). ## Key question 1 — Which UK commercial solar sites have the clearest, most persuasive explanation of PPA vs outright purchase vs asset finance/lease?

### Takeaway
Only a handful of UK sites actually *compare* the routes with numbers; most list "CAPEX / PPA / asset finance" as three cards with no figures. The clearest are Spirit Energy's finance page (comparison table plus four worked project examples with year-one saving, payback and IRR), Ecoaim's PPA article (a 250 kWp system costed three ways), Eden Sustainable's PPA page (rate discount, escalator, term, balance-sheet and end-of-term treatment), Geo Green Power's finance page (tax reliefs plus a three-column comparison table and an FCA disclosure), Absolar's ungated cash-vs-loan-vs-PPA calculator, and Excel Energy's financing page (real p/kWh, term and eligibility thresholds via a named finance partner).

### Inferences
- The persuasive pattern is consistent across the best pages: (1) one-paragraph definition, (2) who funds/owns/maintains, (3) term and pricing mechanics (rate, escalator), (4) end-of-term options, (5) balance-sheet/tax treatment, (6) a comparison table or worked example, (7) "who it suits / who it doesn't", (8) FAQs, (9) a low-friction CTA (quick assessment form or downloadable guide). Spirit, Ecoaim and Eden hit almost all nine; EvoEnergy and Olympus hit only 1–4 and 8–9.
- A two-person installer cannot fund PPAs itself, so the models to copy are the installer-plus-named-funder pages (Photon Energy naming Two Blues Solar/BPCE; Excel Energy naming Smart Ease; Absolar naming NatWest/Lombard) and the honest "when a PPA is right and when it isn't" framing used by Ecoaim, rather than the funder-owned pages (Eden, MTFD).
- Where finance is brokered, the credible sites carry an FCA Introducer Appointed Representative line in the footer (Geo Green Power, Ecoaim, Green Building Renewables, Solar4Good); this is a cheap trust signal a start-up can replicate once it has a broker relationship.

### Gaps
- No site fetched publishes a real side-by-side *numeric* table of PPA vs asset finance vs CAPEX for the same project except Ecoaim (blog article) and Spirit (four different projects); none show a PPA rate range together with an asset-finance APR.
- Solarsense's PPA page ("partnered with the UK's leading financial institutions to provide business customers with a free, fully maintained solar PV system via their power purchase agreement", per search snippet) could not be fetched: both the homepage and the PPA page returned a SiteGround "sgcaptcha" robot-challenge page on 2026-09-28 — [Solarsense PPA page](https://solarsense-uk.com/commercial-renewable-energy-for-businesses/solar-power-purchase-agreement/)
- Olympus Power's homepage footer reportedly references a "PPA Calculator", but no calculator link exists in the raw HTML and /ppa-calculator/ returned HTTP 404; treat as unverified.

## Ranked shortlist and the most realistic templates for a two-person firm

### Takeaway
Top 8 overall: EvoEnergy, Spirit Energy, Excel Energy, Absolar, Geo Green Power, Ecoaim, Perfect Sense Energy, GB NRG. The three most realistic templates for two electrician brothers with subcontract experience and no portfolio are Excel Energy (structure and trust wall on Elementor), Ecoaim (process, sector hub, tone and education at small-firm scale) and Photon Energy (commercial-only positioning, badge wall and partner-funded PPA page on a hosted builder), borrowing the finance-page content pattern from Spirit Energy and Eden Sustainable.

### Inferences
- The two brothers' three years of subcontract commercial experience can be presented the way Excel presents "30 years of combined industry experience" and Spirit presents "136 Years of Combined Experience": as combined experience and named-project types rather than as a portfolio, with a process page and badges carrying the weight until first-party case studies exist.
